# Browser extension: technical context

The Chrome extension in `browser-extension/` brings the app's learning to YouTube on a computer. Commands and
rules are in [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#browser-extension); this page is its source map and boundaries.

Bring DualSub Replay's way of learning from YouTube to the computer: watch real videos with the original subtitle
and its translation together, hear any line again with one click, understand every word, and keep the words worth
remembering.

Like the Android app, DualSub stays free and open source. It has no account, no ads, no tracking, no project server
and no required key. Learners on slow or filtered networks are first-class users: features must still work when a
service is slow or blocked, and must say so plainly when one fails.

The Android app is the reference for learner-facing behavior: colors, word help and practice. The browser
version changes only what the platform needs it to.

## Captions

The YouTube page player's own caption download is the only source. `page/hook.js` runs in the page's world at
document start, wraps `XMLHttpRequest` and `fetch`, and copies replies from `/api/timedtext` that have no `tlang`
(YouTube's own translations are skipped) and are up to 8 MiB. Those requests already carry the page's cookies and
proof-of-origin token, which YouTube now demands. The player only downloads captions while CC is on, so
`ui/content.js` presses CC once per video if it is off, and hides YouTube's own caption window while DualSub's
overlay shows. Only json3, the format the desktop player asks for, is parsed.

## Translation and word help

- Google's batches and the AI's requests start with the part being played (or the address's `t=` time before the
  player jumps), so a video opened from a saved word is translated there first
  ([spec](../specs/2026-11-21-browser-extension-android-look-guide-and-playing-first.md)).
- Caption pieces are joined into sentences before anything else (`sentenceLines`, Android's `sentenceCaptionUnits`;
  [spec](../specs/2026-11-21-browser-extension-whole-sentence-translation.md)). Rows, replay and translation all use sentences, like
  Android's Whole sentence format. Translating pieces alone gave broken Persian.
- Lines are translated in batches of at most 50 texts and 4,000 characters. The first endpoint that answers wins.
- Google's dictionary endpoint (`translate_a/single`) answered 429 or was refused on the owner's network
  (2026-11-21), so meanings come from Wiktionary. The caption language's entries come first; Translingual entries
  are skipped.
- The word card shows the translation as soon as it arrives. Each line's words are translated in one request when
  the line starts playing. A hover starts the dictionary lookup, and the first line's first word opens the
  connection when captions load. Lookups are cached per video (`lookups` in `ui/content.js`).

## AI translation

Toolbar menu → Translate subtitles with → AI translation ([spec](../specs/2026-11-21-browser-extension-ai-subtitle-translation.md)).
Google translates every line, while at the same time `translation/ai.js` batches the sentences into requests of 60
sentences or 6,000 characters, each with the 4 sentences before it as context. The batch at the playing time goes
first. The prompt is Android's, except that a piece of a longer sentence is translated on its own and never moves
meaning into another line's translation (version 3, [follow-up](../specs/2026-11-21-browser-extension-ai-subtitle-translation.md)). Each answered line replaces Google's, Google keeps what the AI leaves
out. A busy service is asked again after 1 s and 3 s, then after a 30 s rest while Google's lines show, as Android
does; other failures stop the AI for that load. The service, key and model live in `chrome.storage.local.ai`
(never synced), and the key is sent only to that service's API host. Answers are kept per sentence under `aic:` keys
in `chrome.storage.local` (prompt version, service, model, languages and text), so only missing sentences are sent.

## Word card

Pronounce uses Google's voice (`translate_tts`, as Android's `GoogleWebSpeech`), played as a `data:` URL on the page,
and the browser's `speechSynthesis` when that fails. A second word clicked in the same line selects the phrase between
the two. English lines are tagged by the service worker with Android's OpenNLP model and analyzed as on Android: WordNet
dictionary forms, CEFR-J levels, IPA, and the grammar points of the clicked word in its sentence
([spec](../specs/2026-11-21-browser-extension-english-grammar-points.md)). The data in `data/learner-data/` is extracted by
`tools/english/build_extension_english_data.py` from the same Maven Central jars as Android's build; the catalogue is generated
from the Android source by `tools/english/build_extension_english_grammar.py`.

## Spoken word

Auto-generated json3 pieces keep their chunk times (`words`). Pieces are cut at sentence ends inside them before
joining ([spec](../specs/2026-11-21-browser-extension-spoken-word-and-sentence-splits.md)). The content script underlines the latest
word whose time has come, every 150 ms.

## Part of speech

`data/words.js` ports Android's `PartOfSpeech` colors and the fallback rules of `LanguageAwareTokenizer` (closed-class
word lists, then suffixes) for English, and its Japanese particle, adjective and verb-ending rules. Words of other
languages get `other`. English lines are then recolored by the tagger once the service worker has analyzed them.
Android's other analyzers (OpenNLP for Spanish and German, Kuromoji, jieba) are not ported yet. Colors are made for dark backgrounds and are mixed 55% toward black on light ones, matching Android's
45% darkening.

## Saved words and practice

`chrome.storage.local.words` is an array, newest first. Each entry holds `id` (word + sentence, so saving the same
word from the same line updates it; an imported word keeps its Android id), `word`, `pos`, `translation`, `meanings`,
`sentence`, `sentenceTr`, `lang`, `target`, `videoId`, `title`, `time`, `end`, `added`, `dueAt`, `intervalMs`, and
the optional `notes`, `base`, `ipa` and `level`. Export and import use Android's JSON backup v1 and Anki TSV
([spec](../specs/2026-11-21-browser-extension-word-card-and-practice-parity.md)), so words move between the phone and the browser. Practice shows due words, oldest first, and
uses Android's intervals: Again 10 min; Hard, Good and Easy 1, 3 and 7 days for a new word, otherwise ×1.2, ×2 and
×3 of the last interval, capped at 10 years.
