# Privacy overview

DualSub Replay is designed without an application account, analytics SDK, advertising SDK, or project-operated server.

## Information handled by the app

- The embedded YouTube website receives normal browsing and playback requests, subject to Google's and YouTube's policies.
- The app requests public caption data from YouTube to build the replayable subtitle timeline. It keeps the caption tracks of up to six recently watched videos in its private cache for a day, so reopening one does not download it again; Android may clear that cache at any time.
- Google ML Kit may download the language models you select. Translation then runs on the device.
- Settings → Translation → Google Translate (online) is on by default: the subtitle text and words being translated are sent to Google Translate (`translate.googleapis.com`, or `clients5.google.com` when that one is refused) with the source and target language, and Google's privacy policy applies to them. This uses Google's free web endpoint, not an official API, so Google may limit or stop it. While it is on, a Chinese word you ask to hear is also sent to Google Translate's speech address (`translate.googleapis.com/translate_tts`) to get its spoken audio. Translations are cached in the app's private cache. Turning the switch off sends nothing more, translates on the device and speaks Chinese with the phone's own voice.
- The app has an optional AI assistant (the button at the top right; More settings → AI assistant turns it off completely). It sends nothing until you add your own API key from an AI service you choose (Google Gemini, OpenRouter, OpenAI, OpenCode Zen or Go, or another OpenAI-compatible address) and ask something. Each question is then sent to that service, together with a short description of the app, the app's current settings and translation status, the app version, and your interface language. "Explain the current line" adds the subtitle line on screen and its translation; "Ask AI about this" adds the problem the app shows, including its technical detail. When you ask about what is being said, the assistant may read the subtitle line on screen and up to two lines before and after it, with their translations, and sends them with that question; a "Read the subtitles on screen" note under its answer shows each time. When you ask it to, the assistant can change subtitle settings, replay, pause or slow down the video, save a word to your vocabulary on the phone, and, after you tap a button, search YouTube, open a YouTube link you typed, or change translation. Pictures and files you add with + go with that question; they stay in memory for the open chat only, and saved chats keep just their names. The service's own terms and privacy policy apply, and you pay it directly if it charges. Video IDs, saved words, Progress, and your YouTube account are not sent.
- Your API key is encrypted with a key kept in the Android Keystore and stored in a separate preferences file that is excluded from Android backups and device transfers, so after a restore or a move to a new phone you add it again. The app shows only its last 4 characters and never sends it anywhere except to the service it belongs to.
- AI chats stay on the phone in the app's private storage and are excluded from backups and device transfers. By default they are kept for 7 days and then deleted automatically; More settings → AI assistant → Chat history offers Off (nothing is saved), 7 days, 30 days, or Forever, and "Delete chat history now".
- The AI assistant's memory: when you tell it something lasting about yourself (your level, a goal, an exam date, how you like explanations) or ask it to remember something, it saves one short note on the phone and shows "Saved to memory" with Undo under its answer. It never saves notes from subtitles, files, pictures or error details by itself; then a note waits for your tap. At most 30 notes are kept, and the instructions you write in More settings → AI assistant → Memory ("Your instructions") are kept with them. Both stay in the app's private storage, are excluded from backups and device transfers, and are sent to your AI service with each question. There you can edit or delete each note or all of them. "Use memory" off stops the assistant reading or saving notes, and a new chat can be started without memory; your instructions are still sent then.
- The first time Japanese subtitles load, the app downloads the Japanese word dictionary (the unchanged 13 MB Kuromoji IPADIC file) from Maven Central (`repo1.maven.org`) or, if that fails, Google's mirror of it (`maven-central.storage-download.googleapis.com`). The request asks only for that file; no subtitle text is sent. The file is checked against a pinned SHA-256 checksum, kept in the app's private storage, and used to split Japanese text into words on the device.
- The app stores the last Browse URL, target language, subtitle text size, and landscape split ratio in local Android preferences.
- Saved words, meanings, subtitle context, video IDs, timestamp ranges, and review schedules are stored in a local SQLite database. They are not sent to an application server. Android's normal app backup may include this database and preferences.
- Progress stores, per day and language, how long videos played in the app and how many videos were watched, in a separate local SQLite database, plus your daily goal in local preferences. Video IDs and titles are not stored for Progress, and nothing is sent to an application server. Android's normal app backup may include this database.
- Study tools (Settings → Study tools) work on the phone. Words you mark as known, and with Automatic known words how often you have seen each word not known yet, are kept per language in the app's private storage (in Android backups, like saved words) and are not sent anywhere. Send to, when you use it, gives each word with its meaning, reading, sentence, translation and a link to its moment in the video to the flashcard app you picked on the same phone: AnkiDroid (after you allow its permission), or AnkiConnect at the phone's own address `127.0.0.1:8765` (the AnkiConnect Android app or another AnkiConnect tool on the phone; nothing leaves the phone); which words were sent is kept in local preferences. Load captions with a page token (beta, Automatic by default) lets the YouTube page ask YouTube (`www.youtube.com`) and Google (`www.google.com/js/th/`) for the same proof-of-origin token YouTube's web player makes, when YouTube sends empty captions (and, after that worked, first on later videos); set it to Off to never do this. The token stays in the page.
- Settings → Ads & sponsors works in the YouTube page on the phone. Skip ads (on by default) only mutes, speeds up and skips the ads the page shows, and sends nothing. Skip sponsors (on by default) asks SponsorBlock (`sponsor.ajay.app`) for the sponsor parts of the open video: the request names only the first 4 characters of the video ID's SHA-256 hash, so SponsorBlock gets the parts of every video sharing them and does not learn which one you watch, though it sees your IP address like any website. The parts are kept in memory only. SponsorBlock's privacy policy applies; turn the switch off to send nothing.
- YouTube tabs (Tabs in the menu) are kept in local Android preferences: each tab's YouTube address, page title, and account number. The Tabs list shows each video's small picture from YouTube's image server (`i.ytimg.com`), the same picture YouTube's pages show; it is kept in memory only. With Settings → YouTube tabs → Use different Google accounts (off by default), Accounts 2 and 3 are separate Android WebView profiles, each with its own cookies and sign-in, stored with the rest of the embedded browser's data and, like it, left out of backups and device transfers.
- Saved words replay their moment in the same embedded YouTube page. The app no longer downloads video clips; the offline clip downloader of earlier versions has been removed.
- Pronunciation sends the selected word and language to the device's preferred Android text-to-speech engine and, if needed, other installed speech engines. It prefers installed offline voices but may try a supported online voice when local speech fails or is unavailable. Online voices process the word according to the speech engine provider's settings and privacy policy. The spoken word is recorded to a temporary file in the app's cache so it can replay instantly. It is deleted when you select or pronounce another word or close the app, and any file left behind is removed the next time a word is spoken.
- The Japanese dictionary is excluded from Android backups and device transfers, because it can be downloaded again. The embedded browser's data, including its sign-in cookies, is excluded too, and so are the AI assistant's API keys, chats, memory and instructions. Saved words, Progress, and preferences stay in backups.
- If you use the experimental Google/YouTube sign-in flow, authentication occurs inside YouTube's embedded web experience and WebView cookies persist locally. They are excluded from Android backups and device transfers, so after a restore or a move to a new phone you sign in again. Google may reject embedded-WebView sign-in.
- Settings → App updates → Tell me about new versions (on by default): once a day when the app opens, it reads two small public files from GitHub: the latest release's `update-info.json` (`github.com` and its download hosts) and the project's remote switches (`raw.githubusercontent.com/hoangkien1703/dual-sub-replay/main/remote-config/switches.json`). The requests carry no account, cookie or identifier, though GitHub sees your IP address like any website. The switches can only turn some features off or show a notice, never add code. Turn the setting off to send nothing to GitHub unless you tap Check for updates. The app's problem log (for Report a problem) stays in its private cache, is never sent by itself, and is left out of backups.
- The app turns on WebView Safe Browsing, so on devices that use Google's WebView, the pages you open are checked against Google's list of unsafe sites.

## What the project does not do

- It does not require an API key or DualSub Replay account. Only the optional AI assistant needs a key, which you get from the AI service yourself; everything else works without one.
- It does not send subtitle text or translation requests to a server operated by this project. Subtitle text goes to Google only while Google Translate (online) is on, which is the default, and to the AI service you chose only when you ask the AI assistant about it.
- It does not download video or audio from YouTube.
- It does not intentionally collect analytics, advertising identifiers, or crash telemetry. Menu → Report a problem shows the recent problems the app noted on the phone (which step failed, Google's answer code and address; never your videos, subtitles or account) and only opens an email to the project's report address (`dualsubreplay.help@gmail.com`) or a GitHub bug report with them; you read, change and send it yourself.

Deleting the app clears its local app data under Android's normal uninstall behavior. You can also clear the app's storage from Android Settings.

This overview describes the current open-source implementation. Review the source and release notes before installing if your privacy requirements are strict.
